How Fresno Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Fresno

The majority of water heater failures we see in Fresno come back to the water itself. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Well water can bring iron, sulfur, and grit into the tank, and a rotten-egg odor in the hot water often means a reaction with the anode rod that a different rod can fix. Older homes often need code upgrades when a heater is replaced: an expansion tank where a check valve or pressure regulator creates a closed system, a properly routed T&P discharge line, a drain pan where a leak could damage finished space, and earthquake straps in areas that require them. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early.
